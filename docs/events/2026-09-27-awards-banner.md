# Banner for the Awards and Titles 2026

For the website strip and the newsletter, for the event on Sunday 27 September 2026 at the Officer's Transit
Hostel, Race Course, Dehradun. Paste a prompt below into Gemini or ChatGPT.

## How to use it

1. **The model draws the artwork only. It never draws the words.** Every prompt ends with a clause forbidding
   text. Mangled lettering and invented Devanagari are what make a banner look machine made.
2. Generate at **16:9**. Crop to **3:1** (1920 x 640) for the website strip and **2:1** (1200 x 600) for the mail.
3. Keep the **left third quiet**. That is where the words go.
4. Generate three or four and pick one. Ask for a change in plain words ("less mist", "move the grove right").

## Prompt A, recommended: a Garhwal painting

The Pahari miniature tradition is Uttarakhand's own, so the banner belongs to the place rather than to a stock
library.

```
A wide horizontal painting in the manner of an eighteenth-century Garhwal-school Pahari miniature, mineral
pigments on handmade paper. A grove of mature deodar and banyan stands across the right two thirds of the frame:
pale trunks, canopies built from small leaves drawn one by one in deep green, moss and ochre. Round three of the
trunks a cotton raksha sutra of red and orange thread is tied in a plain knot, two short loose ends hanging.
Behind the grove, low Himalayan foothills recede in flat washes of soft grey green, in the manner of miniature
painting and not of photography: no vanishing point, no depth of field, no blur. The left third is quiet open
ground in bare paper tone, cream and softly mottled, where nothing competes for attention. Even daylight, warm
but not golden hour. A fine gold hairline runs as a border a little inside the edge of the painting, hand drawn
and slightly uneven. Palette: deep forest green, moss, cream and unbleached paper, antique gold, and one red
thread. Flat and matte, with visible brush texture and the small irregularities of hand painting.
No text, no letters, no numerals, no signature, no logo, no people.
```

## Prompt B: a documentary photograph

```
A wide documentary photograph of a deodar forest in the Garhwal Himalaya on an overcast September morning. Tall
straight trunks fill the right two thirds; round several of them a red and orange cotton raksha sutra is tied in
a plain knot. Thin mist between the trees, wet bark, undergrowth of ferns. The left third opens onto a quiet
clearing with even grey light and nothing in it. Shot on a 50 mm lens at eye level, natural light only, muted
colour, fine grain, true to life. No sun flare, no glow, no strong contrast, no colour grading, no drone view.
No text, no letters, no numerals, no logo, no people.
```

## Prompt C: pressed leaves on paper

The most restrained of the three, and the easiest to lay type over.

```
A wide photograph taken straight down onto a sheet of handmade cream paper with a deckled edge. On the right,
arranged with care and plenty of space between them, lie pressed peepal and banyan leaves in green and ochre, a
small coil of red and orange cotton thread, and one thread tied in a plain knot. The left half of the paper is
empty. Soft even daylight from a window, gentle shadows, the paper's texture and fibres visible. Muted and
natural colour. Nothing styled or symmetrical; it should look like a working table, not a flat lay.
No text, no letters, no numerals, no logo, no hands.
```

## Add this to any prompt

```
Avoid: lens flare, glow, bokeh, floating particles, confetti, trophies, ribbons, medals, gold coins, fireworks,
HDR, 8k, hyperdetailed, cinematic lighting, trending on artstation, watermark, border frame with ornament,
stock-photo gloss.
```

## The words go on top, in real type

Set these in the site's own two fonts, both free on Google Fonts, so the banner and the website look like one
thing: **Tiro Devanagari Hindi** for the name and the event, **Mukta** for the details. Colours from the site:

| Use | Colour |
|---|---|
| Deep green ground | `#0C1710` |
| Cream text on the artwork | `#F3EEE2` |
| Gold, for the date | `#E0B347` |
| Thread red, for one rule or dot | `#AF472C` |

Five lines, no more. The six award names belong on the invitation card, not on the banner.

**English**

```
Vrikshabandhan Abhiyan (Registered)
Vrikshabandhan Awards and Titles 2026
Sunday, 27 September 2026, from 10:30 am
Officer's Transit Hostel, Race Course, Dehradun
vrikshabandhanabhiyan.in
```

House rules, as on the site: sentence case, no middle dots, no em dashes. The logo sits small in a top corner,
never stretched.

## In the mail

Do not put these words inside the image. Use the artwork as a picture at the top of the mail and set the words
below it as ordinary text, so they are readable when a reader's inbox blocks images, and selectable everywhere.

## One question for you

The card reads "Vrikshabandhan / Registered Awards & Titles / 2026". "Registered" looks like it belongs to the
organisation's name rather than to the awards. "Vrikshabandhan Awards and Titles 2026" reads cleanly and is used
above. Yours is the final word.
